# Goodnight TV: concept v10 (approved direction, Oct 20, 2026)

**The question every judge will ask:** *Do I believe this gets a child to sleep on time?*
Not "is it cute" or "is it clever". If the answer is no, Design, Impact and Idea all drop together.

---

## 1. Judging the current concept (v8) honestly

| What a judge believes | What a judge doubts |
|---|---|
| A routine with a fixed order helps; the steps are the real ones (teeth, pajamas, lights) | **The fight is still there at the start.** A parent still has to switch off the child's show and open Goodnight TV. That *is* the battle, and the app starts after it |
| The screen getting darker is visible and measurable | **Why would a 4-year-old leave the TV to brush, then come back?** Nothing pulls them through the routine except a bear asking |
| The ending goes to the parent: warm idea | **Two transitions, not one.** The story ends on the sofa, then the child still has to go to bed. That's a second fight |
| No AI forced in; honest claims | **No proof it works:** no family tried it; the only number is computed screen light |
| | **The character and motion look home-made** next to Headspace, Moshi or Waterllama. It reads as "hackathon", not "award" |

**Belief today: 2.5 / 5.** Charming, but the hard moments of bedtime (switching the show off, going to bed) are exactly the ones it doesn't handle.

---

## 2. Why bedtime goes wrong, and what actually helps (the evidence we build on)
| What delays sleep | What the research says | What Goodnight TV must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Light in the hour before bed** | Bright light 1 h before bed cut preschoolers' melatonin by ~88%, still suppressed 50 min after (Akacem et al., 2018, n = 10). Even low light suppressed it (Hartstein et al., 2022) | Make **the whole room** dim, not just the screen: the big light goes off early, and the TV fades to black |
| **No routine, or a different one every night** | A nightly routine is linked, dose by dose, with earlier bedtimes, faster sleep and over an hour more sleep (Mindell et al., 2015; association, parent-reported) | The same steps, in the same order, every night, visible as pictures |
| **The fight to end screen time** | Ending the screen is where conflict happens; a third party ending it (not the parent) is the classic advice. *We found no trial on warnings themselves, so we don't claim one* | **The TV ends TV time itself**, at the planned time, with a warning first |
| **Resisting going to bed** | Behavioural methods (e.g. the "bedtime pass") work by making staying in bed rewarding (small trials) | **A reason to want to be in bed:** the story's ending only exists there |
| **Too little sleep before school** | Sleep need by age (AASM: 10–13 h at 3–5, 9–12 h at 6–12) | Bedtime worked back from the school alarm; lights-out time recorded every night |

We claim only what the app does (less light, a fixed routine, an automatic end, a recorded lights-out time), never "better sleep".

## 3. Concept v9: what changes

### A. Bedtime arrives by itself, so the parent isn't the bad guy
- At **plan time minus 5 minutes**, an Alexa routine speaks over whatever is on: *"Five minutes, then Bramble's coming."*
- At plan time, the routine opens Goodnight TV (Fire TV routine action "Open Goodnight TV"). **The show ends because the TV ends it.**
- The parent sets this up once, from the companion's guide ("Make bedtime automatic").
- **To verify on the stick:** a scheduled routine opening a sideloaded app. Fallback: the parent says "Alexa, open Goodnight TV". Bramble's arrival is still the end of TV time, not the parent's "no".
- **Demo moment:** a bright show playing, Alexa's warning, and then the screen *itself* turns to dusk and Bramble arrives. The pain and the twist in 15 seconds.

### B. The fireflies are the light, and the child is in charge of letting it go
- The jar holds **the evening's light**: one firefly per step.
- Each finished step: the child presses OK, the lid lifts, a firefly peeks out, loops once and flies up to become a star. The screen visibly loses that much light.
- **The real big light is a step too:** "Grown-up, switch off the big light." The room goes dark, and on screen the cottage window goes dark at the same moment.
- At the end, the child lets the last firefly go. The room is now as dark as it should be an hour before sleep.
- *Why a child cooperates:* each step has an immediate, beautiful reward (their firefly, their star), plus a visible "how much is left". That's Waterllama's loop, aimed at bedtime.

### C. One transition, not two: the ending lives in bed
- The TV tells the story up to the very last scene, in the dark: *"…and then something wonderful happened. But that part only happens in bed. Your grown-up has it."*
- The last four lines are on the parent's phone. The child **wants** to go to bed to hear the ending.
- The final transition becomes a reward instead of a fight (the same lever as the bedtime pass: being in bed is where the good thing is).

### D. Patience, never pressure
- During a step done away from the TV, Bramble waits: dim scene, slow breathing. After 2 minutes he hums softly; after 3, he calls the child by name, once.
- No timers racing, no failure states, no lost stars. A missed night is just an unlit star on the week.
- Every step ends with a press, which also keeps Fire TV awake for the routine (its 5-minute idle display-off, friction log #1).

### E. Predictable, with a little wonder
- **Same every night:** same steps, same order, same goodbye. Predictability is the point.
- **One small new thing each night:** tonight's story hero, who joins Bramble's hill and greets the child tomorrow. Kids look forward to bedtime because something of theirs is waiting.

### F. Proof the app can collect by itself
- Every night it records **bedtime start → lights out** (minutes) and the lights-out time against the school plan.
- **The morning view:** "Dark at 8:04 · 10 h 56 before school · 9 minutes faster than Monday."
- **For the submission:** room lux at the sofa per step (measured), and if possible 1–2 real families for a few nights, reporting only real numbers.

### G. Craft: from "home-made" to award level
Code-drawn shapes can be top-notch if they are **rigged and lit**, not stacked.
- **A rigged Bramble:** 9 separate parts (body, head, two ears, two arms, the jar, eyes, mouth, cap with pom), each with a pivot, moved with springs.
  - **Idle:** breathing (chest rises, head follows), the pom swings, an ear flicks every ~7 s, two-stage blinks, eyes follow focus.
  - **Talking:** head bobs on the voice's rhythm; four mouth shapes from the audio level.
  - **Feelings:** proud (puffs up when a firefly flies), patient (sways while waiting), sleepy (heavy lids, slow blinks, a full yawn with arms stretching).
- **The silhouette:** a gently drooping cap, one ear slightly folded, rounder cheeks, a softer muzzle, two-tone shading on every shape. Same flat style, more life.
- **Light as the material:**
  - The jar is the brightest thing on screen and rim-lights Bramble's belly and chin.
  - As fireflies leave, the rim light fades: **the light literally leaves the character.**
  - Fireflies are glowing particles with soft halos, flicker and wobbling paths.
- **Depth:** three parallax layers drift a few pixels; the drift slows with the evening and stops at the end. Stillness is the last frame.
- **Benchmark:** Headspace for Kids and Moshi for character warmth, Waterllama for the reward moment, Calm for light and stillness. Any frame that wouldn't fit beside them gets redone.

### H. Cut, to make room for craft
- Story worlds beyond Animals; the potty step; the green noise level picker (Soft only).
- Fewer screens on first launch (age and wake-up only; steps default on, editable on the phone).

## 5. Decisions for you
1. **Approve v9** (or change it) before any code.
2. **The Alexa auto-open:** can you check on your phone whether an Alexa routine can be scheduled at a time and use the Fire TV action "Open Goodnight TV"? If yes, it's the demo's opening. If not, voice launch.
3. **A real bedtime test:** is there a child (yours, family, a friend's) who could try it for 1–3 evenings before Thursday? Even one real night beats any projection.
4. **Craft budget:** I recommend spending most of the remaining build time on the rigged Bramble, the light and the firefly moment, and cutting section H. Agree?

## 6. v10: personalised by AI, grounded in sleep medicine, invisible to the child
The child's screen stays exactly as simple as above. AI works only where it helps, never in the child's controls and never in the timing path.

| What | Evidence it rests on | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Choices first, then only wind-down** | Calm, predictable "positive routines" before bed are a recommended treatment for bedtime problems (AASM practice parameters, Morgenthaler et al. 2006; review: Mindell et al. 2006) | The three story picks move to the **start**, right after Bramble's hello. After that, the child only does real steps and presses OK. Energy only goes down |
| **Tonight's story knows tonight** | Worry and excitement at bedtime keep children awake; telling a calm story about the worry is classic bedtime advice (bibliotherapy). We claim no effect | The parent types one line on the phone ("Leo lost his first tooth", "nervous about the school trip") or taps a suggestion. **Amazon Nova** writes the story from the child's picks + that line, inside the hand-written wind-down shape (4 shrinking scenes, a yawn, slow breathing, an ending that only happens in bed). Strict checks: word counts, no exclamation marks, a banned-words list; any failure falls back to the hand-written story |
| **Off the critical path** | – | The story is written and voiced **while the child brushes and changes** (minutes of slack). Bedtime never waits on the cloud |
| **The parent gets one small, proven next step** | **Faded bedtime** (start when the child actually falls asleep, then move it 15 min earlier at a time) is one of the treatments with the best evidence for bedtime resistance (Piazza & Fisher; AASM practice parameters) | Every morning the app looks at the real log (start time, minutes per step, dark time vs. the school plan). A rule picks **one** change ("start 10 minutes earlier", "lay the pajamas out before Bramble arrives"); Nova phrases it warmly on the phone. Nothing changes on the TV without the parent |
| **Bramble remembers** | Predictability with a little novelty keeps kids engaged | Tomorrow's greeting mentions tonight's hero and tonight's thread ("Did the dragon help with your wobbly tooth?") |

**Alexa (checked Oct 20):** routines can run on a schedule and include a Fire TV "open app" action (typed command). There's no confirmation that it opens a *sideloaded* Vega app, and one report of an "open app" routine showing search results instead. Test on the family's own setup; if it fails, the parent launches the app by remote or voice, and it goes in the friction log.

**Real test:** the builder's own children, several evenings this week. Report only real numbers (minutes from start to dark, lights-out vs. plan, what the kids said).

Sources: [AASM practice parameters, Morgenthaler et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17068980/) · [Mindell et al. 2006 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17068979/) · [Akacem et al. 2018, Physiological Reports](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5835497) · [Hartstein et al. 2022, via ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/01/220127104208.htm) · [Mindell et al. 2015, via AASM](https://aasm.org/study-shows-that-children-sleep-better-when-they-have-a-nightly-bedtime-routine/) · [Bedtime pass study, JABA 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1702334) · [Alexa routines on Fire TV, TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/11/18/amazon-brings-alexa-routines-to-fire-tv/)

## 7. v11 focus line (Oct 20): the TV keeps kids awake, so let the TV put them to bed

**One line:** *Every other show keeps your child up. This one puts them to bed.*

| The pain, in evidence | What it means for us |
|---|---|
| TV is already part of bedtime: about three in four parents of 5–10-year-olds said TV was part of the regular bedtime routine, and 15% said their child fell asleep in front of it at least twice a week (Owens et al., Brown, 1999, n = 495) | We don't fight the habit; we take the TV slot and change what the TV does in it |
| Parents believe TV helps: 30% of parents with a TV in a young child's bedroom put it there *to help the child fall asleep* (Kaiser Family Foundation, 2006, n = 1,051) | The intent is right; the content is wrong. Same screen, opposite job |
| It doesn't help: screen time was linked to later bedtimes and shorter sleep in ~90% of 67 studies (Hale & Guan, 2015, review, associations) | Our screen gets darker every step and ends black: the opposite of a show |
| Changing what's on can work: a randomised trial (n = 565 preschoolers) that swapped evening and violent content for calmer media cut the odds of sleep problems (Garrison & Christakis, *Pediatrics* 2012) | Calm content, no evening stimulation; the effect faded when the programme stopped, so the routine must be nightly |
| A routine works fast: in a trial, a 3-step nightly routine improved young children's sleep and mothers' mood within weeks (Mindell et al., *SLEEP* 2009); consistency is linked dose by dose with earlier bedtimes and more sleep (Mindell et al., 2015) | Same steps, same order, every night: Setup makes it once, the TV repeats it |

**What we claim:** less screen light, a fixed routine, a screen that goes black by itself, a lights-out time we can log. **Not** "better sleep" until real nights say so.

Sources: [Owens et al. 1999 (Brown)](https://www.brown.edu/Administration/News_Bureau/1999-00/99-023.html) · [Kaiser Family Foundation 2006](https://www.benton.org/headlines/study-shows-how-kids-media-use-helps-parents-cope) · [Hale & Guan 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25193149/) · [Garrison & Christakis 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3428755) · [Mindell et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2675894/) · [Mindell et al. 2015, via AASM](https://aasm.org/study-shows-that-children-sleep-better-when-they-have-a-nightly-bedtime-routine/)
